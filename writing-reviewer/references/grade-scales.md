# Grade Scales and Conversions

**Convert by band, not by number.** A UK 72% is an excellent First; a US 72% is a C-. Always map the *quality band* (see `rubrics.md` §1) into the user's system. If the user's institution publishes its own scale, use that.

If you don't know the user's system, infer it from cues (spelling, "module", "semester", "coursework", named institution, "GPA"). Once the system is known or confidently inferred, report **only in that system**, since a conversion into another system is noise for the reader. Give UK % and US letter together only when there are no cues at all.

Use the band names exactly as the system does. UK undergraduate 40–49 is a **Third (pass)**; say "a bare pass" at 40–42 if that helps the writer understand. In UK master's schemes the pass mark is usually 50, so 40–49 is a **Fail** (some schemes allow compensation or condonement at 40–49; note that only if relevant).

## Notes on specific systems

**UK (and systems modelled on it, e.g., Ireland, Malta, Hong Kong, many Commonwealth universities):**
- Undergraduate: First ≥70, 2:1 60–69, 2:2 50–59, Third 40–49, Fail <40.
- Master's: Distinction ≥70, Merit 60–69, Pass 50–59, Fail <50.
- Marks above 80 are uncommon and above 90 are rare in essay-based subjects. A mark in the high 60s is a strong result.
- Some institutions use different pass marks or letter grades mapped to percentages — ask or follow their published scheme if named.

**US:**
- Typical letter bands: A 93–100, A- 90–92, B+ 87–89, B 83–86, B- 80–82, C+ 77–79, C 73–76, C- 70–72, D 60–69, F <60.
- Grade distributions in US higher education are compressed toward the top; a B is an ordinary result, not a poor one. Still apply the band descriptors — don't give an A to competent work.

**International Baccalaureate:**
- Internal assessments and the Extended Essay have their own criteria (EE: A–E overall from criterion points). If the user mentions IB, use the relevant IB criteria names where you know them and say the mapping is approximate.

**Australia/NZ:** HD 85–100, D 75–84, Cr 65–74, P 50–64, F <50 (varies by institution).

**Continental Europe:** many systems use 1–10 (NL, BE; 5.5/6 pass, 8+ very good, 9+ rare), 1–5 or 1–6 (DE: 1.0 best, 4.0 pass), 0–20 (FR: 10 pass, 14+ very good, 16+ rare), or 18–30 (IT: 18 pass, 30 cum laude). Map by band and state the conversion is approximate.

**Secondary school exams (GCSE 9–1, A-level A*–E, AP 1–5, SAT essay etc.):** use the official mark scheme's assessment objectives if you know them; otherwise map by band relative to the age group, and say it's an estimate.

## Reporting format

Report as: **Mark: 64% (Upper Second / 2:1) · ≈ US B+** and, when confidence is limited, a range: **likely 62–66%**.
