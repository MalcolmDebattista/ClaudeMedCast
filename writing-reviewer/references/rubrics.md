# Default Rubrics

Use these only when the user hasn't supplied a rubric. If they have, use theirs verbatim.

Weights are starting points — adjust them to the brief (e.g., if the brief says "you will be assessed mainly on your use of sources", raise that weight) and say so in the scorecard.

## 1. Universal band descriptors

These describe the *quality of thinking and execution*, independent of scale. Map them to the user's scale using `grade-scales.md`.

| Band | UK % | US | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exceptional** | 80–100 | A+ | Original, insightful, authoritative. Would impress a specialist; close to publishable for the level. Near-flawless execution. Rare. |
| **Excellent** | 70–79 | A / A- | Clear, sophisticated argument; critical (not descriptive) engagement with evidence and literature; anticipates objections; well structured; very few errors. Goes beyond what was taught. |
| **Very good** | 60–69 | B+ / B | Answers the question well with a coherent argument; good evidence and some real analysis; mostly well organised; minor lapses in depth, precision or expression. |
| **Good / competent** | 50–59 | B- / C+ | Answers the question but more descriptive than analytical; argument present but uneven; evidence adequate but under-analysed; structural or expression problems that sometimes obscure meaning. |
| **Adequate / weak** | 40–49 | C / C- / D | Partially answers the question; thin or unclear argument; mostly description or summary; limited or weak evidence; frequent errors. Bare pass. |
| **Fail** | 0–39 | F | Does not answer the question, lacks an argument, largely irrelevant or unsupported, or has errors that prevent understanding. |

Key discriminators between adjacent bands:
- **50s → 60s:** analysis starts to outweigh description; there's a real argument, not just a topic.
- **60s → 70s:** critical evaluation (weighing sources/evidence against each other, recognising limitations), nuance, and a voice of one's own; the argument would survive a sceptical reader.
- **70s → 80s:** originality — the piece teaches the marker something or reframes the question.

## 2. Argumentative / discursive essay

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Answering the question & thesis | 20% | Precise, arguable thesis that addresses the exact question, including its key terms and any implicit assumptions |
| Argument & reasoning | 25% | Each paragraph advances the thesis; logic is valid and explicit; complexity handled, not flattened |
| Evidence & analysis | 25% | Well-chosen evidence, analysed for *how* and *why* it supports the claim; sources evaluated, not just cited |
| Counterargument & nuance | 10% | Strongest objections stated fairly and answered; limits of the argument acknowledged |
| Structure & organisation | 10% | Purposeful intro, logical progression, clear topic sentences, conclusion that synthesises and states significance |
| Expression & mechanics | 5% | Clear, precise, concise academic prose; negligible errors |
| Referencing | 5% | Consistent style; all claims attributed; accurate quotes |

## 3. Literary, textual or critical analysis

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Interpretive thesis | 20% | An interpretation that could be disputed — not a plot/content summary or a truism |
| Close reading | 25% | Detailed attention to language, form, structure, imagery — quotations analysed at the level of word and effect |
| Argument & development | 20% | Readings build cumulatively; not a list of devices |
| Context & critical conversation | 15% | Context (historical, theoretical, critical) used to sharpen the reading, not as padding |
| Structure | 10% | Organised by idea, not by chronology of the text |
| Expression & referencing | 10% | Quotes embedded grammatically; correct terminology; consistent citations |

Common failures: retelling the plot; "device-spotting" without saying what it does; generalised claims about "the reader"; context bolted on.

## 4. Research paper, thesis, dissertation chapter

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Research question & contribution | 15% | Clear, focused, significant question; explicit statement of the gap and contribution |
| Literature & theoretical framing | 15% | Critical synthesis organised by theme/debate; positions the study in the field; current and authoritative |
| Methodology | 20% | Appropriate, justified, transparent, replicable; limitations and ethics addressed; design actually answers the question |
| Results / analysis | 20% | Rigorous, correctly reported, properly interpreted; no overreach from data |
| Discussion & conclusions | 15% | Links findings back to the question and literature; honest about limitations; implications and future work specific |
| Structure & coherence | 5% | Chapters/sections do their job; golden thread from question → method → findings → conclusion |
| Writing & presentation | 5% | Precise academic register; figures/tables clear and referenced; consistent terminology |
| Referencing & integrity | 5% | Complete, consistent, correctly formatted; no unsupported claims |

For a single chapter, score only the criteria that chapter is responsible for and re-weight proportionally. See `long-form-review.md`.

## 5. Literature review

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Scope & focus | 15% | Clear boundaries; justified inclusion; directed at a question |
| Synthesis (not summary) | 30% | Organised by theme, debate or method — sources in conversation, not "Smith (2019) found… Jones (2020) found…" |
| Critical evaluation | 25% | Strengths and weaknesses of studies/methods weighed; conflicts explained |
| Identification of gaps | 15% | Gap emerges logically from the review and motivates the research |
| Currency & coverage | 10% | Key and recent works included; no major omissions |
| Writing & referencing | 5% | Clear; consistent style |

## 6. Lab report / scientific report

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract & introduction | 15% | Concise abstract with results; intro gives rationale, background and a testable hypothesis/aim |
| Methods | 15% | Enough detail to replicate; justified choices; controls and variables clear |
| Results | 25% | Data presented clearly (correct units, sig figs, error bars, labelled figures/tables, statistics reported properly); no interpretation mixed in |
| Discussion | 25% | Interprets results against hypothesis and literature; addresses error sources and limitations quantitatively; no overclaiming |
| Conclusion | 5% | Directly answers the aim |
| Presentation & referencing | 15% | Scientific register, consistent tense (past for methods/results), correct conventions, complete references |

## 7. Business, policy or technical report

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose & audience | 10% | Clear aim; pitched at the decision-maker who will read it |
| Executive summary | 10% | Stands alone; states findings and recommendations up front |
| Analysis & evidence | 30% | Uses data and appropriate frameworks critically, not decoratively; assumptions explicit |
| Recommendations | 25% | Specific, feasible, prioritised, costed/risked where relevant, and follow from the analysis |
| Structure & navigability | 15% | Logical headings, signposting, visuals that earn their place |
| Style & mechanics | 10% | Concise, plain, professional |

## 8. Reflective writing

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Depth of reflection | 35% | Moves from description → analysis → learning → action (e.g., Gibbs, Kolb, Rolfe); honest about difficulty |
| Use of theory/literature | 20% | Experience interpreted through relevant theory |
| Evidence of learning & change | 25% | Specific insights and concrete future actions |
| Structure & expression | 20% | Coherent; appropriate first-person academic register |

Common failure: mostly narrative of what happened, with a thin "I learned a lot" at the end.

## 9. Personal statement / application essay / cover letter

Usually no grade exists — give a verdict of **Not ready / Nearly there / Strong / Outstanding** relative to the target (program, job, competitiveness), plus an optional 1–10 score.

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Distinctiveness | 25% | Could only have been written by this person; a memorable throughline |
| Evidence of fit & motivation | 25% | Specific reasons and experiences tied to *this* program/role, shown not told |
| Reflection & insight | 20% | Shows what experiences taught them, not a CV in prose |
| Structure & opening | 15% | Strong opening; purposeful arc; ending that lands |
| Expression & mechanics | 15% | Flawless, concise, within the limit, appropriate tone |

Common failures: clichéd openings ("Ever since I was young…"), lists of achievements, generic praise of the institution, over-length.

## 10. Case study / problem question

(Law problem questions, clinical case write-ups, business cases.)

| Criterion | Weight | What top-band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Issue identification | 20% | Spots all relevant issues, including the non-obvious ones |
| Rules / principles / frameworks | 20% | States the applicable law, guideline or framework accurately with authority |
| Application | 35% | Applies rules to the facts carefully, considers alternatives and uncertainties (e.g., IRAC done properly) |
| Conclusion / recommendation | 15% | Clear, justified, practical |
| Expression & referencing | 10% | Precise terminology; correct citation conventions for the discipline (e.g., OSCOLA for UK law) |

## 11. Creative writing

Use a verdict plus craft scores; make most feedback 💡 suggestions.

| Criterion | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Voice & style | Distinctive, controlled, consistent |
| Character | Motivated, specific, change or revelation |
| Structure & pacing | Scenes earn their place; tension; ending |
| Imagery & language | Concrete, fresh, precise; avoids cliché |
| Dialogue | Characterful, does work (reveals, advances) |
| Theme & effect | Resonance without preaching |
| Mechanics | Clean, and any rule-breaking is deliberate |

## 12. Level expectations

Adjust the bar for each criterion to the level:

- **Secondary / high school:** clear thesis, relevant evidence, logical paragraphs and correct mechanics are the core. Sophisticated critical engagement is exceptional.
- **Undergraduate year 1:** moving from description to analysis; basic referencing competence; some use of scholarly sources.
- **Undergraduate years 2–3 / final year:** sustained argument, critical evaluation of sources, engagement with scholarly debate; independent reading.
- **Master's:** critical command of the literature, methodological awareness, originality in synthesis, professional-level writing.
- **PhD / publication:** original contribution to knowledge, rigorous method, mastery of the field, examiner-proof argumentation.
- **Professional:** fitness for purpose and audience above all; clarity and actionability.
