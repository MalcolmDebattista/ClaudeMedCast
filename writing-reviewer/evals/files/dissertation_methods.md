# Chapter 3: Methodology

## 3.1 Introduction

This chapter outlines the methodology used to answer the research question: *How do newly qualified nurses experience moral distress during their first year of practice in acute NHS settings?* The chapter describes the research design, sampling, data collection, data analysis and ethical considerations.

## 3.2 Research Design

A quantitative cross-sectional survey design was adopted for this study. Quantitative research is objective and allows results to be generalised, which is why it is the best approach for all nursing research. A survey was chosen because it is quick and cheap, and allows a large number of participants to be reached (Polit and Beck, 2017).

The Moral Distress Scale-Revised (MDS-R) (Hamric et al., 2012) was used to measure moral distress. The MDS-R is a 21-item instrument where participants rate the frequency and intensity of morally distressing situations on a 0–4 Likert scale. It has been widely used and is valid and reliable.

## 3.3 Sampling

Participants were recruited using convenience sampling through a Facebook group for newly qualified nurses. The inclusion criteria was: registered nurse, qualified within the last 12 months, working in an acute setting. A total of 212 people clicked on the survey link and 64 completed the survey, giving a response rate of 30%. The sample was mostly female (91%) and the average age was 24.

## 3.4 Data Collection

Data was collected using an online questionnaire hosted on Google Forms between March and April 2024. The questionnaire included demographic questions and the MDS-R. Participants were also asked one open question: "Is there anything else you would like to tell us about your experience?"

## 3.5 Data Analysis

Data was analysed using SPSS version 28. Descriptive statistics were used to describe the sample. A Pearson correlation was used to test the relationship between months since qualification and moral distress scores. The correlation was significant (r = -0.21, p = 0.000), which proves that moral distress is caused by inexperience and decreases as nurses become more experienced.

The responses to the open question were analysed using thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006). Three themes were identified: staffing, end-of-life care and support from managers.

## 3.6 Ethical Considerations

Ethical approval was not required as the study was an anonymous survey. Participants were told that by completing the survey they consented to take part. Data was stored on the researcher's personal laptop.

## 3.7 Limitations

The main limitation was the small sample size. However, 64 participants is enough to generalise the results to all newly qualified nurses in the UK.
