# To what extent has social media changed the nature of political participation?

In today's fast-paced world, social media has become a huge part of everyones life and it has completely changed politics. This essay will discuss how social media has changed political participation. It will look at the positives and negatives of social media and politics.

Social media first became popular in the 1990s when Facebook was launched by Mark Zuckerberg. Since then, it has grew to have billions of users. According to a study, 87% of young people now get all of their political news from TikTok. This shows that social media is the main way young people engage with politics and that traditional forms of participation, like voting, are no longer important to them.

One of the main ways social media has changed participation is through "clicktivism". Clicktivism is when people like or share political posts instead of doing real activism. Morozov (2011) argues that this is "slacktivism" and that it makes people feel good without changing anything. I think this is true because liking a post does not actually do anything. Many people share posts about climate change but they dont actually change there behaviour. Therefore clicktivism is bad for democracy.

However, social media has also helped movements like the Arab Spring. In 2011, protesters in Egypt used Facebook and Twitter to organise protests which led to the fall of Mubarak. This proves that social media causes revolutions. Without social media the Arab Spring would never of happened. Similarly, the Black Lives Matter movement started on Twitter in 2013 with the hashtag #BlackLivesMatter and it has since become one of the biggest movements in history.

Another important point is echo chambers. Echo chambers are when people only see opinions they agree with. Sunstein (2017) says that the internet creates "Daily Me" where people only see news they want. This is bad because people become more extreme. Its a big problem for democracy because people cant have proper debates anymore. Pariser (2011) also talks about filter bubbles which is similar.

Social media has also given politicians a new way to talk to voters directly. Donald Trump used Twitter a lot during his presidency and this allowed him to bypass the traditional media. Politicians can now talk directly to there supporters without journalists. This is both good and bad, it is good because it is more direct but it is bad because there is no fact-checking.

There is also the issue of fake news and misinformation. During the 2016 election there was lots of fake news on Facebook. A study by Johnson and Peters (2019) in the Journal of Digital Politics found that fake news changed the result of the election by 12%. This shows how dangerous social media can be for democracy.

In conclusion, social media has changed political participation in many ways. It has positives like helping movements such as the Arab Spring and BLM, but it also has negatives like clicktivism, echo chambers and fake news. Overall I think social media has had a big impact on political participation and it is important that we continue to study it.
